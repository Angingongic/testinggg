Romanian verbs are grouped into four conjugation classes.

Verb endings change to reflect person and number.

The infinitive typically ends in -a, -ea, -e, or -i.

According to RomanianPod101, the present tense is most common for everyday speech.

Auxiliary verbs are used to form compound tenses.

The verb "a fi" (to be) is irregular and essential.

"A avea" (to have) also has irregular forms.

Romanian verbs express aspect through perfect and imperfect tenses.

The subjunctive mood is introduced with "să."

Imperative forms provide commands or requests.

Reflexive verbs use reflexive pronouns like "se."

The conditional mood expresses possibility or polite requests.

"A vrea" (to want) helps form the future tense with "voi."

Some verbs have stem changes in certain conjugations.

The past participle forms the compound perfect.

RomanianPod101 stresses practicing conjugations aloud.

Many verbs derived from French maintain similar meanings.

The imperfect tense describes habitual actions in the past.

To form the simple perfect, you use endings like -ai, -ă.

The pluperfect indicates actions completed before another past event.

The gerund ends in -ând or -ind and shows ongoing action.

The supine is used in expressions like "de făcut."

Verb aspect can also be shown with prefixes and suffixes.

Modal verbs such as "a putea" express ability.

Pay attention to irregular verbs when studying with RomanianPod101.

Phonetic changes may occur at the boundary of stem and ending.

Verbs of motion often pair with prepositions.

In colloquial speech, some endings may be shortened.

Verb drills help internalize patterns.

Listening to dialogues will show verbs in natural context.
