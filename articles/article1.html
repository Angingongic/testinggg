Romanian nouns come in masculine, feminine, and neuter genders.

Gender influences both articles and adjective endings.

Masculine nouns often end in a consonant.

Feminine nouns typically end with -ă, -ea or -ie.

Neuter nouns take masculine endings in the singular.

In the plural, neuter nouns adopt feminine endings.

RomanianPod101 emphasizes learning gender early to avoid mistakes.

Each noun has definite and indefinite forms.

The indefinite article appears before the noun.

The definite article is usually a suffix attached to the noun.

For masculine singular nouns, the ending is -ul or -le.

Feminine singular nouns take -a or -ua as the definite ending.

Plural masculine nouns get the definite suffix -ii or -i.

Plural feminine nouns end in -le for the definite form.

Romanian also uses cases like nominative and accusative.

The genitive-dative case shows possession or indirect object.

Some nouns form the plural irregularly.

Many animal names follow masculine patterns.

Proper names for men are masculine.

Proper names for women are feminine.

Occupations can change form depending on the gender of the person.

The ending -că often marks feminine nouns derived from masculine forms.

RomanianPod101 provides lists of common irregular plural forms.

Nouns that describe objects may be neuter.

Abstract nouns often use feminine endings.

Family terms include both regular and irregular plurals.

Learning noun patterns speeds up vocabulary acquisition.

Paying attention to article endings helps with comprehension.

Memorizing gender with each new noun prevents confusion later.
