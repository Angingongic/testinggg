Romanian cardinal numbers start with "unu" for one.

"Doi" means two, and "trei" means three.

Numbers agree in gender when counting masculine or feminine nouns.

"Unu" is used for counting neuter nouns as masculine.

Ordinal numbers add "-lea" for masculine or "-a" for feminine.

RomanianPod101 lists common ordinal forms like "al doilea."

Tens are formed with the root plus "zeci," such as "douăzeci."

Hundreds use "sută" as in "o sută."

For 1000, you use "mie."

Compound numbers join with "și," meaning "and."

"Unsprezece" means eleven.

"Douăzeci și unu" means twenty-one.

Numbers above twenty combine tens and units.

Thousands group with "de," like "două mii de oameni."

Gender agreement matters for "un" vs. "o."

Fractions often use "împărțit la."

RomanianPod101 encourages practicing numbers with everyday objects.

Phone numbers are usually read in groups of two.

Years are read as whole numbers.

The decimal point is a comma in Romanian notation.

Percentages use "la sută."

Ordinals preceding nouns trigger the definite article.

"Prima dată" means "the first time."

For large numbers, spaces are used as thousand separators.

In casual speech, people may shorten numbers.

Dates typically use day-month-year order.

The week starts with Monday in Romania.

Learning numbers helps with prices and directions.

RomanianPod101 has listening exercises for numbers.

Frequent practice ensures quick recall.
