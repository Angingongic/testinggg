Adjectives in Romanian usually follow the noun.

They agree in gender, number, and case with the noun they modify.

The masculine singular form is the dictionary entry.

Many adjectives have feminine forms ending in -ă or -e.

In the plural, masculine adjectives often end in -i.

Feminine plurals usually end in -e.

RomanianPod101 suggests memorizing adjectives with their gender patterns.

Adjectives of nationality follow similar gender changes.

Colors act as adjectives and must agree with nouns.

Some adjectives are invariable, meaning they don't change form.

Comparative forms use "mai" plus the adjective.

The superlative is formed with "cel mai" or "cea mai" followed by the adjective.

Irregular comparisons also exist, such as "bun" becoming "mai bun."

Adjectives describing shape and size are common in everyday speech.

Possessive adjectives like "meu" and "ta" come after the noun.

RomanianPod101 highlights that adjectives of material use the preposition "de."

The particle "foarte" intensifies the adjective meaning "very."

Negative forms add "nu" before "este" or other verbs to deny a quality.

When multiple adjectives modify a noun, they keep the same agreement.

Short adjectives may precede the noun for emphasis.

Ordinal numbers are adjectives and agree with the noun.

The adjective "fost" indicates former status.

Participles used as adjectives show completed action.

Some adjectives derive from verbs with the suffix "-tor."

Learning collocations from RomanianPod101 improves fluency.

Comparative structures using "decât" mean "than."

In spoken Romanian, adjectives may drop the final "e."

Slang adjectives add color to informal conversation.

Adjectives can also be used as nouns when context is clear.

Practicing with real dialogues is the best way to master adjective placement.
