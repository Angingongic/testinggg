Romanian has several past tenses including compound perfect, simple perfect, imperfect, and pluperfect.

The compound perfect is most common in conversation.

It uses the present tense of "a avea" plus the past participle.

For example: "am vorbit" means "I spoke."

The simple perfect appears mainly in literature.

It has endings like -ai, -ă, -ă, -arăm.

RomanianPod101 suggests focusing on the compound perfect first.

The imperfect describes repeated or ongoing past actions.

Endings for the imperfect include -am, -ai, -a.

The pluperfect expresses an action completed before another past event.

It uses the auxiliary "a avea" in the imperfect plus the past participle.

Many verbs have irregular past participles.

To form negatives, simply add "nu" before the auxiliary.

Questions invert the auxiliary and subject or use intonation.

Reflexive verbs keep the reflexive pronoun before the auxiliary.

Past tense forms can convey nuance of aspect.

The past participle for first conjugation verbs usually ends in -at.

Second conjugation verbs often form participles in -ut.

Third conjugation verbs typically have -ut or -it.

Fourth conjugation participles often end in -it.

RomanianPod101 explains differences between regional usage of the simple perfect.

The imperfect is often used with phrases like "în timp ce."

The pluperfect can show cause in subordinate clauses.

Many adverbs of time combine with the past tenses.

Irregular verbs must be learned separately.

In storytelling, the simple perfect adds dramatic effect.

The compound perfect is rarely used in subordinate clauses starting with "dacă."

Verb aspects can be expressed with prefixes.

Practicing with short narratives helps solidify past tense forms.
